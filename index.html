How Value-Based Care Actually Works

A structured overview of the ecosystem, patient lifecycle, data infrastructure, and measurement framework — and how a decade of public health surveillance work maps directly to healthcare analytics.

The Value-Based Care Ecosystem

Value-based care flips the traditional healthcare payment model — rewarding physicians for keeping patients healthy rather than for volume of services. ACO enablers sit at the center of this ecosystem, providing the data infrastructure, analytics, and contracts that help independent primary care practices participate and succeed.

🏥
Who delivers

Independent Primary Care Practices

The frontline of healthcare. Physicians who see patients, manage chronic diseases, coordinate specialist referrals, and order tests. Historically paid per service — now rewarded for outcomes.

🏛️
Who regulates & pays

CMS — Centers for Medicare & Medicaid Services

Sets ACO program rules, benchmarks, quality measures, and payment terms. Runs MSSP — the Medicare Shared Savings Program — the flagship value-based care contract vehicle.

🤝
The enabler

ACO Enablement Companies

Organizations that provide data analytics, workflow tools, payer contracts, and coaching to help independent practices participate and succeed in value-based care programs at scale.

📋
The contract

MSSP ACO

Medicare Shared Savings Program. Practices manage an attributed patient panel against a cost benchmark. Spend less than benchmark + hit quality targets = share the savings with CMS. The core contract mechanism for most ACO programs.

⚖️
The old model

Fee-for-Service

Pay per visit, test, and procedure. More volume = more revenue. No incentive to coordinate care or prevent illness. CMS is actively moving toward value-based alternatives.

🎯
The new model

Value-Based Care

Pay for keeping patients healthy and reducing unnecessary costs. Coordination is financially rewarded. Prevention over treatment. Better data = better outcomes = shared savings.

Key Terminology

Benchmark

Expected Cost Threshold

CMS sets expected spending for each ACO's patient population based on historical costs. Spending below benchmark generates savings. Above benchmark = potential losses (two-sided tracks).

Attribution

Patient Assignment

Patients are assigned to an ACO based on which primary care physician they visit most. The ACO is then responsible for that patient's total cost of care — including specialists and hospitalizations.

Quality Measures

HEDIS & eCQMs

Clinical performance metrics ACOs must hit — diabetes control, blood pressure management, cancer screenings. Poor quality scores reduce shared savings even if cost targets are met.

Risk Adjustment

HCC Coding

Hierarchical Condition Categories. Adjusts payments based on patient complexity — sicker patients generate higher risk scores and higher expected costs. Accurate documentation is critical.

Care Gap

Missed Preventive Care

A preventive service or chronic disease management task that should have happened but didn't. Example: diabetic patient with no HbA1c test in 12 months. Closing gaps improves quality scores.

Shared Savings

The Financial Reward

When an ACO spends less than benchmark and meets quality thresholds, CMS shares the difference. Aledade takes a portion; the rest goes to the physician practice. This is how practices profit from keeping people healthy.

The Most Expensive Cost Drivers

🏥
Biggest single cost event

Hospitalization

General inpatient: $15K–30K. ICU: $50K–100K+. Surgical: $30K–150K+. One avoided admission can generate more savings than dozens of office visits — the primary target of all ACO analytics.

🔄
Expensive failure mode

30-Day Readmission

$15K–25K on top of the original stay. Signals a failure of care coordination. CMS penalizes hospitals for high readmission rates. ADT event notification is the key intervention tool.

🚨
Preventable waste

Avoidable ER Visits

$2K–3K for care that a $150 primary care visit could handle. Preventable ER visits cost the US $32 billion annually. Often driven by access problems — patients who can't get same-day appointments.

💊
Lifetime cost accumulator

Chronic Disease

Diabetes: $16,750/year vs $5,800 for non-diabetics. ESRD (dialysis): $90K+/year. The tragedy — early intervention costs hundreds. Downstream complications cost hundreds of thousands.

🧠
Underappreciated driver

Social Determinants of Health

Housing instability raises hospitalization rates 30%. Food insecurity raises costs 27%. Lowest income quartile costs 3–4× more than highest. SDOH screening is now part of the ACO toolkit.

💉
Fastest-growing category

Pharmacy Costs

GLP-1 medications: $10K–15K/year. Specialty drugs: $50K–500K+. Non-adherence multiplies costs — patients who stop taking medications are hospitalized at dramatically higher rates.

The Data Foundation

Impact analytics is only as good as the data infrastructure underneath it. Before you can measure whether interventions worked, you need clean, normalized, centralized data from multiple sources with different latencies, formats, and quality standards.

Data Collection — What Needs to Come In
📋

Claims Data — The Backbone

CMS sends Medicare claims data with a 3–6 month lag. Every office visit, hospitalization, ER visit, specialist referral, and drug prescription for every attributed patient. Broad coverage but shallow clinically — tells you what was billed, not what happened clinically.

🏥

EHR Data — The Clinical Detail

Lab results, vital signs, diagnoses, medications, care plan notes flowing from each practice's EHR. Near real-time but narrow — only what happened at that specific practice. Unstructured physician notes require NLP to extract structured signal.

⚡

ADT Feeds — The Early Warning System

Admission, Discharge, Transfer notifications from hospitals in real time. Aledade knows a patient was admitted within hours — not months — enabling immediate care coordination and discharge planning intervention.

💊

Pharmacy Data — Adherence Signals

Part D prescription claims showing what drugs were dispensed, when, and whether refills are on track. Critical for medication adherence measurement — a key driver of chronic disease outcomes and hospitalization risk.

📊

Patient-Reported Data — SDOH & Experience

CAHPS surveys, SDOH screenings, patient satisfaction scores. Less structured and harder to aggregate but increasingly important for quality measures and identifying social risk factors that predict poor health outcomes.

Normalization Challenges

Entity Resolution
Is "Dr. Sarah Johnson NPI 1234567890" in claims the same as "S. Johnson MD" in EHR? Patient matching across sources — your similarity matching work applied to healthcare entities.
Code Normalization
Type 2 diabetes coded as E11.9 in one system, E11 in another, free text in a third. Must normalize to ICD-10, SNOMED, RxNorm — the regulatory taxonomy layer of healthcare data.
Attribution Reconciliation
Which patients belong to which practice? Changes quarterly as patients change care patterns. Continuous data quality challenge that directly affects benchmark calculations.
Temporal Alignment
Claims: 3–6 month lag. EHR: near real-time. ADT: real-time. Measuring whether an intervention worked requires aligning events across different time horizons correctly.

Key Coding Systems

ICD-10 — Diagnoses
E11.9 = Type 2 diabetes. I10 = Hypertension. N18.3 = CKD stage 3. Every diagnosis in claims and EHR.
CPT — Procedures
99213 = office visit. Every service billed to Medicare requires a CPT code. Drives utilization analytics.
LOINC — Lab Observations
4548-4 = HbA1c. 33914-3 = eGFR. 8480-6 = Systolic BP. Standard codes for every lab test and vital sign measurement.
FHIR — Interoperability
Modern REST API standard for health data exchange. Patient, Observation, Condition, Encounter resources. 21st Century Cures Act mandates EHR support.
NPI — Provider Identity
National Provider Identifier. Unique ID for every physician, hospital, pharmacy. Links provider activity across claims — your merchant ID equivalent.
Key Clinical Metrics — What You'll Work With in Projects
🩸

HbA1c — LOINC 4548-4

Glycated hemoglobin. Reflects average blood sugar over 3 months. ≥6.5% = diabetes. Rising trend across encounters is a classic early intervention signal. HEDIS measure: % of diabetics with HbA1c < 8%.

🫘

eGFR — LOINC 33914-3

Estimated Glomerular Filtration Rate. Measures kidney function. <60 for 3+ months = chronic kidney disease. Declining eGFR is one of the clearest longitudinal risk signals — serious, actionable, and expensive if missed.

❤️

Blood Pressure — LOINC 8480-6 / 8462-4

>130/80 = hypertension stage 1. Persistently elevated BP combined with other risk factors drives cardiovascular hospitalization. HEDIS measure: % of hypertensives with BP <140/90.

The Measurement Framework

Once data is centralized and normalized, how do you actually measure whether Aledade's interventions are working? The framework follows the same structure as compliance impact analytics — establish baseline, measure intervention, attribute outcomes, benchmark against peers.

Metric Data Required How to Measure The Causality Challenge
Hospitalization Avoidance
Primary target of ACO model
Highest Value 		Inpatient claims · Historical baseline · Comparison population · Risk adjustment data Hospitalizations per 1,000 attributed patients/year vs benchmark and vs pre-Aledade trajectory. Risk-adjust for patient complexity before comparing across practices. Difference-in-differences vs non-Aledade practices. Propensity score matching to find comparable patients. Interrupted time series showing trajectory change at intervention start.
30-Day Readmission Rate
Care coordination signal
High Value 		Inpatient claims with admission/discharge dates · ADT post-discharge follow-up data · 7-day PCP visit claims Readmissions within 30 days per 1,000 discharges. Track whether post-discharge follow-up appointments (within 7 days) reduce readmission rates — measurable intervention-to-outcome chain. ADT notification → care manager outreach → PCP follow-up → readmission avoided. Each step in the chain is measurable. The causal chain is clearer here than for primary prevention.
Care Gap Closure Rate
HEDIS quality driver
High Volume 		EHR lab data · Claims for screening procedures · Outreach tracking · Clinical guidelines for gap definitions Full funnel: gaps identified → patients outreached → contacts made → gaps closed → time to closure. % of diabetics with HbA1c <8%, % hypertensives with BP <140/90, % colonoscopies completed. Outreach attribution is cleaner — you can track whether care manager contact directly preceded gap closure. Compare closure rates for reached vs not-reached patients.
Chronic Disease Control
Longitudinal outcome
Leading Indicator 		EHR lab data over time · LOINC-coded observations · Multiple measurements per patient · Longitudinal patient records % diabetics with HbA1c <8% trending over time. Distribution shift — not just average but full distribution moving. EHR lab data is 3–6 months ahead of claims — a leading indicator for HEDIS submissions. Patient-level trajectory analysis — is HbA1c improving or worsening across successive measurements for a given patient. Compare trajectories for patients receiving active care management vs not.
Shared Savings Attribution
Financial proof of value
Business Critical 		CMS benchmark calculations · Total claims spend · Quality score · Attribution file · Practice-level breakdown Savings = (Benchmark − Actual Spend) × Attribution count. Adjusted by quality score if below threshold. Broken down by practice, intervention type, and patient segment to identify what's driving performance. CMS benchmark formula is complex and contested — ACPT calculation can inaccurately predict spending. Aledade is actively advocating for fixes. Savings projections must account for benchmark methodology uncertainty.
Practice Benchmarking
Peer comparison
Engagement Driver 		All metrics above across all practices · Practice characteristics (size, specialty, geography, patient complexity) · CMS public use files Compare each practice to peer cohort — same specialty, same market, same size tier. Rank on cost, quality, and care gap closure. Identify high performers and what they do differently. Risk adjustment critical here — a practice with a sicker panel should have higher costs. Must adjust for patient complexity and SDOH before comparing across practices. Cherry-picking concerns in peer design.
Care Manager Productivity
Operational impact
Efficiency 		Outreach activity logs · Contact records · Gap closure outcomes · Care manager time tracking Patients outreached / care manager. Contact rate (% reached). Gap closure rate (% of contacts who closed a gap). Time from gap identification to closure. Improvement over time as tools improve. Directly analogous to analyst efficiency metrics — items reviewed per analyst before and after the pre-scored queue. Same productivity measurement framework, different human reviewer.
